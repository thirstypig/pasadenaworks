> **TL;DR** — The Pew Research Center tracked the actual browsing of 900 US adults through 68,879 Google searches in March 2025, of which 12,593 produced an AI summary. Where a summary appeared, users clicked a traditional result on 8% of visits, against 15% where none appeared. Clicks on a link inside the summary happened on 1% of visits, and sessions ended considerably more often on pages carrying a summary: 26% against 16%. Those four numbers collectively constitute the most honest picture currently available of what AI search actually does to a small business's traffic.

Almost everything written about AI search is either a vendor's projection or a publisher's complaint, whereas this is neither: it constitutes observed behavior from people who agreed to have their browsing recorded, which makes it considerably more valuable than the remainder of the genre combined.

## What the study measured

The Pew Research Center analyzed data from **900 US adults** who consented to sharing their browsing activity, across the period **1–31 March 2025**.

Within that window the dataset contained **68,879 unique Google searches**, of which **12,593** produced an AI summary — roughly one search in five, or 18% overall, and approximately six in ten respondents — **58%** — encountered at least one of them.

That constitutes a genuine methodology carrying a stated sample, a stated period and a stated volume, which is precisely what the vendor material in this area consistently lacks.

## The four findings that matter to you

**Clicks on results roughly halve.** Users who encountered an AI summary clicked a traditional search result on **8%** of visits, whereas users who encountered no summary clicked on **15%** — approximately twice as frequently.

**Almost nobody clicks inside the summary.** A click on a source link within the AI summary occurred on just **1%** of visits to pages carrying one, and this is the finding that ought to reset expectations, because a considerable proportion of the advice currently being sold is aimed at achieving citation inside those summaries.

**Sessions end more often.** Browsing ended on **26%** of pages with an AI summary, against **16%** of pages displaying only traditional results, because the answer satisfied the person and they consequently stopped looking.

**It is not yet most searches.** At 18%, AI summaries appeared on fewer than one search in five throughout the study period, which is substantial without constituting the entirety of search, and a proposal treating it as the entirety of search is overstating the case considerably.

## What this means for a local business

The first temptation is to read this as catastrophe and the second is to read it as irrelevant, and both interpretations are mistaken in precisely the same way, because they disregard what category of query is actually involved.

A summary is most likely to satisfy somebody asking a general informational question, which is exactly the category of article traffic that was never going to convert particularly well anyway. A person searching for a plumber who can come today, a restaurant open now, or a price for a specific service still needs to reach somebody, and a summary cannot complete that transaction.

So the reasonable response is not to stop writing but to change what you write, since content whose entire value consisted of answering a general question is demonstrably worth less than it previously was. Pages that answer a question only you can answer — your prices, your availability, your specific process, the thing about your neighborhood — are worth what they always were, because a summary cannot substitute for them.

## How to read your own numbers against this

The pattern appearing in your Search Console will be the one described here, and understanding that in advance prevents it from being misinterpreted.

Stable impressions accompanied by declining clicks constitutes the expected signature rather than evidence that something has broken. Separate it by query type wherever you can, because informational terms losing clicks while commercial ones hold is the study's finding reproduced in your own account, and it tells you where to put effort rather than telling you to panic.

And treat the 1% figure as precisely the correction it represents: being cited inside a summary is worth having and it is emphatically not worth restructuring your business around, because on the published evidence it produces a visit one time in a hundred.
