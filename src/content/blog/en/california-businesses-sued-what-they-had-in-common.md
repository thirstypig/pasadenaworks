> **TL;DR** — The obvious theory is that businesses with inaccessible websites receive letters, and it does not survive contact with the published data. WebAIM's annual survey of one million home pages found detected WCAG failures on 95.9% of them, averaging 56 errors per page, so having failures cannot be what distinguishes a target. What the targets share is being straightforward to find, having something to transact, and possessing no record of remediation. Two of those three you cannot and should not change, whereas the third represents approximately a week's work.

The instinctive reading of accessibility litigation is that it finds the worst websites, which would be reassuring if it were true, because you could examine your own and estimate your risk accordingly. The published data renders that reading untenable the moment you actually look at it.

## Almost every site has the failures being cited

WebAIM conducts an annual automated survey of the top one million home pages, and the 2026 edition identified detected WCAG 2 failures on **95.9%** of them, an increase from 94.8% the preceding year.

The average page carried **56.1** detected errors, and across the million pages the study counted **56,114,377** errors altogether, and the distribution is remarkably concentrated: six issue types account for **96%** of everything detected — low-contrast text on 83.9% of pages, missing alternative text on 53.1%, missing form labels on 51%, empty links on 46.3%, empty buttons on 30.6% and missing document language on 13.5%, and those same six categories have led the list for seven consecutive years running.

Read that against the litigation counts and the theory collapses: roughly nineteen out of twenty sites carry detected failures, while only a few thousand federal cases are filed annually. Having failures is therefore the baseline condition of the web rather than a distinguishing characteristic of a defendant.

## What the targets actually share

If the failures themselves do not select the target, something else necessarily does, and that something else is thoroughly unglamorous.

**Being findable.** Somebody has to encounter the site in order to file about it, and that correlates with ranking well, advertising, and maintaining a complete profile — which is to say, with the marketing actually working. This is an uncomfortable fact to state plainly, and it is emphatically not a justification for making yourself less findable.

**Having a transaction.** A site where somebody can book, order, pay or submit a form presents a barrier with a consequence attached. A brochure site carrying only a telephone number presents considerably less of one, and here again the transaction is the entire point of having the site in the first place.

**Having no record.** This is the only one of the three genuinely within your control, and it is precisely the one businesses habitually neglect. A business that can produce a dated list of what it fixed and when is in a different negotiating position from one that cannot, regardless of the current state of the site.

## The California specifics

California's route differs from the national picture in a manner worth understanding precisely rather than approximately.

California federal courts saw four website accessibility filings in 2025, against 1,021 in New York, because California appellate courts have held that online-only businesses fall outside the ADA — while demand letters and state-court claims continue regardless. A national filing chart consequently tells a Pasadena business very little about its own genuine exposure.

The practical consequence is that the thing most likely to arrive is a letter rather than a complaint, and that a letter is answered with a remediation record rather than with a certificate.

## What this means you should actually do

Fix the six things, because they are demonstrably the six things, and they are identical to the six that the DOJ-referenced guidance would have you address anyway.

Contrast amounts to a handful of values in a theme, and alternative text gets written as you touch individual pages. Form labels are a markup change, and empty links and buttons are usually an icon lacking an accessible name. Document language is a single attribute on a single line, so none of that constitutes a rebuild, and collectively they cover the overwhelming majority of whatever any automated scan — including the one attached to a demand letter — will report.

Then write down precisely what you did and when, because that record is the only one of the three shared characteristics you get to change, and it is the one that matters when something eventually arrives.
