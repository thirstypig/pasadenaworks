> **TL;DR** — The obvious theory is that businesses with inaccessible websites get letters, and it does not survive the data. WebAIM's annual survey of one million home pages found detected WCAG failures on 95.9% of them, averaging 56 errors per page, so having failures cannot be what distinguishes a target. What the targets share is being easy to find, having something to transact, and having no record of remediation. Two of those three you cannot and should not change; the third is a week's work.

The instinctive reading of accessibility litigation is that it finds the worst websites, which would be reassuring if it were true, because you could look at your own and estimate your risk. The published data makes that reading untenable as soon as you look at it.

## Almost every site has the failures being cited

WebAIM runs an annual automated survey of the top one million home pages, and the 2026 edition found detected WCAG 2 failures on **95.9%** of them, up from 94.8% the year before.

The average page carried **56.1** detected errors, and across the million pages the study counted **56,114,377** errors in total. And the distribution is remarkably concentrated: six issue types account for **96%** of everything detected — low-contrast text on 83.9% of pages, missing alternative text on 53.1%, missing form labels on 51%, empty links on 46.3%, empty buttons on 30.6% and missing document language on 13.5%. Those same six categories have led the list for seven consecutive years running.

Read that against the litigation counts and the theory collapses, and roughly nineteen out of twenty sites have detected failures, and a few thousand federal cases are filed each year. Having failures is the baseline condition of the web, not a distinguishing feature of a defendant.

## What the targets actually share

If the failures do not select the target, something else does, and the something else is unglamorous.

**Being findable.** Somebody has to encounter the site to file about it, and that correlates with ranking well, advertising, and having a complete profile — which is to say, with the marketing working. This is an uncomfortable fact to state plainly, and it is emphatically not a reason to make yourself less findable.

**Having a transaction.** A site where somebody can book, order, pay or submit a form presents a barrier with a consequence attached, and A brochure site with a telephone number presents less of one. Here again, the transaction is the entire point of having the site in the first place.

**Having no record.** This is the only one of the three that is genuinely within your control, and it is the one businesses neglect, and A business that can produce a dated list of what it fixed and when is in a different negotiating position from one that cannot, regardless of the current state of the site.

## The California specifics

California's route differs from the national picture in a way that is worth understanding precisely rather than approximately.

California federal courts saw four website accessibility filings in 2025, against 1,021 in New York, because California appellate courts have held that online-only businesses fall outside the ADA — while demand letters and state-court claims continue. So a national filing chart tells a Pasadena business very little about its own exposure.

The practical consequence is that the thing most likely to arrive is a letter rather than a complaint, and that a letter is answered with a remediation record rather than with a certificate.

## What this means you should actually do

Fix the six things, because they are the six things, and they are the same six the DOJ-referenced guidance would have you address anyway.

Contrast is a handful of values in a theme, and alternative text is written as you touch pages. Form labels are a markup change, and empty links and buttons are usually an icon without a name. Document language is one attribute on one line, and none of that is a rebuild, and together they cover the overwhelming majority of what any automated scan — including the one attached to a demand letter — will report.

Then write down what you did and when, and that record is the only one of the three shared characteristics you get to change, and it is the one that matters when something arrives.
