> **TL;DR** — Nobody can submit a business to an assistant and nobody can buy placement in one, so any proposal that implies otherwise is describing a product that does not exist. What you can do is make sure the relevant crawlers are permitted to read your site, state the facts an assistant would need in plain text rather than in an image or a PDF, and keep the sources those assistants lean on — your listing, your reviews, ordinary web pages that mention you — accurate and consistent.

The question arrives now in roughly the form that "how do I get to the top of Google" arrived twenty years ago, and it attracts the same category of vendor. The mechanics are less mysterious than the marketing around them, though they are also less controllable, and being honest about the second part matters.

## Start by not blocking them

The most common reason a business is absent from an assistant's answer is dull: something on the site prevents the assistant's crawler from reading it.

Different assistants use differently named robots, and they are documented publicly — OpenAI, for instance, publishes which of its crawlers do what, including the one that fetches pages to support answers with links. Look at your own `robots.txt` and establish what it currently permits, because plenty of sites carry a blanket rule somebody added years ago for unrelated reasons, and a rule blocking everything unfamiliar will block these too.

This is a genuine decision rather than an automatic yes. Some publishers deliberately exclude AI crawlers, which is a defensible position for a business whose product is its writing. For a plumber, a dentist or a restaurant it is usually the wrong trade, since the whole objective is to be findable.

## State the facts plainly, in text

An assistant answering "who repairs tile roofs in Alhambra" is assembling a response from material it can read, and material it cannot read does not exist for this purpose.

That means the service area, the services, the hours, the license number and whether you take a particular insurance should appear as text on a page rather than inside an image, a scanned PDF or a graphic on the homepage. It also means writing them as direct statements — "we repair tile roofs in Alhambra, Monrovia and San Gabriel" — rather than as marketing atmosphere that never quite names the service.

This is the same advice that helps a human skim-reading on a telephone, which is a useful sign it is not a trick.

## The fundamentals did not change

Google's own documentation is unusually direct on this and is worth taking at face value: there are "no additional requirements to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode, nor other special optimizations necessary," and specifically "you don't need to create new machine readable files, AI text files, or markup to appear in these features."

That statement is narrower than the whole field, since it describes Google's features rather than every assistant. It is nonetheless the clearest published position available, and it directly contradicts a substantial amount of what is currently being sold. When somebody proposes a special file or markup as necessary for AI visibility, ask where the requirement is documented, and notice whether the answer is a page or a tone.

## Feed the sources they actually use

Assistants lean heavily on material that already exists about you elsewhere, which means the highest-return work is frequently not on your own site.

A complete and accurate Google Business Profile matters, because it is the structured record of what you do and where. Reviews matter, both as a signal and as text that describes your business in other people's words. Consistent details across the directories you appear in matter, since contradictory hours and addresses give an assistant no basis for confidence. And a mention on a genuinely relevant local page — a chamber listing, a local news piece, a supplier's stockist page — does more than another paragraph on your own site.

## What you cannot control, said plainly

You cannot guarantee an appearance, you cannot buy one, and you cannot reliably reproduce one, since asking the same question twice can produce different answers with different sources.

That is an uncomfortable thing to be told by somebody in this trade, and it is the accurate position. Anybody offering guaranteed placement in an assistant's answer is either misunderstanding the mechanism or counting on you to. The realistic goal is to be one of the businesses that could reasonably be named, and then to be named some of the time.

## What is worth doing this month

Check `robots.txt` and decide deliberately which crawlers you permit. Put your service area, services and hours in text on a page. Complete the Business Profile. Fix the three directories with the wrong telephone number.

Then ask the assistants your own customers' questions and see what comes back. That last step costs nothing and is more informative than most reports on the subject, not least because it tells you which competitor is currently being named instead of you.
