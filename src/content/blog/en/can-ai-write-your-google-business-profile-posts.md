> **TL;DR** — An assistant can draft these perfectly well, and drafting was never the bottleneck. The bottleneck is having something specific and currently true to say, which is exactly the thing an assistant cannot supply. Use it to turn a real fact — a genuine offer, an actual event, a seasonal change in what you do — into a tidy hundred words, and do not use it to manufacture weekly filler, which costs you the credibility the profile was accumulating.

Business Profile posts occupy an odd position within local marketing, in that they are easy to produce, they appear in a place customers genuinely look, and they are also the single most common place where a small business publishes something nobody wanted to read.

An assistant makes producing them substantially faster, which is helpful only in the case where the thing being produced was worth producing in the first place.

## What these posts are actually for

Google documents what may be posted and in what form, and reading that once will save you from most of the mistakes.

The useful mental model is that a post is a short, dated announcement attached to your listing — an offer, an event, a piece of news, a product. It sits with the profile that already carries your hours, location and reviews, and it is read by somebody who has already found you and is deciding whether to act.

The composition of that audience matters considerably, because this is not a channel for attracting strangers; it is a channel for converting somebody who is already looking at your listing. A post that says "quality service you can trust" tells that person nothing, whereas one saying "closed 4 July, normal hours from 5 July" or "winter tire changeover, $89, no appointment needed before 9am" answers the question they actually had.

## Where an assistant helps

Given a real fact to start from, an assistant is genuinely good at all of the work surrounding it.

It will turn your two-line note into a tidy post of the right length, produce three variations so you can pick a tone, adapt a single announcement into four sensible versions across your languages, and remind you to include the detail you always forget, which for most businesses is either the price or the end date.

That is a real saving, particularly for a business publishing in English, Spanish and Chinese, where the alternative is either three separate writing efforts or three months of only publishing in one language.

## Where it costs you

The failure is specific and it is worth naming precisely: an assistant asked to "write a post for a plumbing business" will produce something fluent, generic and completely untethered from your week.

A post of that description is measurably worse for you than publishing nothing at all. It occupies the slot where a customer expected news, it signals that nobody here has anything specific to say, and repeated weekly it trains people to stop reading. Google's own position on AI content applies straightforwardly — the objection is never authorship, it is producing material with little value for the reader.

The sharper risk, and the one that does lasting damage to a business, is outright invention of things that are not true. An assistant asked to make a post more appealing will readily add an offer you are not running, a timescale you cannot meet, or a superlative you cannot support. Anything published on your profile is a statement by your business, and "the tool wrote it" is not a defense a customer will accept.

## A workable routine

Decide first whether anything is true this week that a customer would want to know. If nothing qualifies, then publish nothing at all, since an empty week is not in any sense a problem.

When something is true, write it down as bare facts — what, when, how much, what to do next — and have the assistant format it rather than originate it. Read the result specifically for claims you did not make, since that is where the errors live and they are easy to miss because everything around them is accurate.

Then translate the same post rather than generating three independent ones, so the Spanish and Chinese versions say what the English one says instead of drifting into three slightly different offers.

## The honest assessment

This is one of the better uses of an assistant in a small business, precisely because the task is short, structured, and easy to check.

It is also one where the tool tempts you toward the wrong behavior, since it makes weekly publishing effortless and weekly publishing is not the goal. The goal is that somebody looking at your listing finds something useful and current, and four good posts a year beat fifty-two forgettable ones by a margin that is not close.
