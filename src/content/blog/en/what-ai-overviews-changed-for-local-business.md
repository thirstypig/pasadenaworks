> **TL;DR** — The requirements did not change: Google states that appearing in AI Overviews needs no special files, markup, or optimization beyond ordinary search visibility. What did change is measurement. Search Console now carries a separate report for generative AI features, and it counts **impressions only** — no clicks, no average position, and AI Overviews and AI Mode combined into one figure. Knowing that limitation is most of what a small business needs from this subject.

When AI Overviews began appearing above ordinary results, the reaction from the marketing industry was close to unanimous, and it was that everything had changed. Two years on, that claim deserves the same scrutiny you would give any prediction sold alongside a service, which is to say a careful look at what the people running the system actually document.

## What did not change, according to Google

The requirements are the easiest part to settle, because Google has written them down in plain terms rather than leaving them to inference.

There are, Google's documentation states, "no additional requirements to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode, nor other special optimizations necessary." The same page rules out the specific products being sold against this anxiety: "you don't need to create new machine readable files, AI text files, or markup to appear in these features. There's also no special schema.org structured data that you need to add." The one condition it does impose is the familiar one, namely that a page "must be indexed and eligible to be shown in Google Search with a snippet."

That is a short paragraph to have absorbed an enormous amount of commentary. If your pages were eligible for ordinary search results before, they remain eligible now, and the technical work that would improve one improves the other.

## What genuinely did change is what you can see

The substantive difference for a small business is not in what you must do but in what you are able to observe afterwards, and this is where a certain amount of honest disappointment is warranted.

Search Console reports on generative AI features separately, and the report is narrower than most people expect. It counts impressions, which Google defines as the number of times links to your site were shown to somebody inside a generative AI feature. It does not report clicks, it does not report average position, and it combines AI Overviews and AI Mode rather than distinguishing between them.

Sit with that for a moment, because it has a practical consequence. You can establish that Google showed your link inside an AI answer, and you cannot establish from that report whether anybody followed it. The traffic that does arrive still lands in your ordinary analytics, but the connection between an impression in an AI feature and a visit to your site is not something the report draws for you.

Anyone quoting you a monthly figure to "optimize for AI Overviews" and promising to report on the results is promising to report on something Google does not currently expose in that detail. That is worth asking about directly, and the answer will tell you a good deal about the proposal.

## The shift is in intent, not in mechanics

Where the change does register for a local business is subtler than a ranking drop, and it concerns which questions still send somebody to your website at all.

A person asking what time a hardware store opens, or whether a particular chemical is safe on a granite countertop, may well have that answered without visiting anybody's site. A person deciding which of three shops to drive to on a Saturday morning is doing something different, and that decision has historically required looking at the businesses themselves — their photographs, their reviews, their sense of whether they are the sort of place that will help.

The first category of question was never worth much to you, even when it did produce a visit. Somebody who arrives, reads one line, and leaves was never going to become a customer. The questions that matter to a local business are the ones where the answer is a choice between named places, and those are precisely the questions where being named, described accurately, and corroborated elsewhere still determines whether you are in the running.

## What to actually measure

Because the generative AI report gives impressions without clicks, the useful approach is to stop trying to isolate that channel and instead watch the outcomes that have always mattered.

Look at whether telephone calls and direction requests on your Google Business Profile are holding steady or declining, since those actions happen without a website visit at all and are unaffected by however somebody arrived at the decision. Watch your overall organic traffic as a trend across quarters rather than weeks, because a single month tells you almost nothing at this scale. And read the generative AI impressions report for what it genuinely offers, which is confirmation that Google is willing to show your pages inside these features, rather than as a number to be optimized.

If those outcomes are steady, the change has not damaged you, whatever the commentary says. If they are declining, the cause is worth investigating properly, and it is at least as likely to be a competitor who improved, a profile that has gone stale, or a seasonal pattern, as it is to be an AI feature.

## The honest summary

For most small businesses in a physical location, AI Overviews changed the shape of the traffic more than the size of the opportunity, and it changed measurement more than either. The work that made you findable continues to make you findable, Google says so explicitly, and the newest thing in the toolbox is a report that counts impressions.

That is a duller conclusion than the one being marketed, and it has the advantage of being what the documentation says.
