> **TL;DR** — For most small businesses, a genuine website runs $1,500–$6,000 as a one-time build from a freelancer or small shop, or $0–$30 monthly if you construct it yourself on a DIY platform such as Squarespace or Wix. Agencies charge considerably more for what is frequently the same result. AI website builders and "vibe coding" tools can produce a live page for almost nothing up front — but cheap to launch and cheap across three years are not the same question, and that one remains genuinely unsettled.

If somebody quotes you $300 for a website, or alternatively $30,000, both numbers can be entirely correct, because they are answering different questions. What follows describes what genuinely drives the resulting price, and precisely where AI tools belong within that arithmetic.

## What actually moves the price

Three separate factors drive the overwhelming majority of the cost difference:

* **Custom construction versus template.** A templated site, whether assembled through a self-service builder or by a freelancer working from an existing theme, costs measurably less because nobody is constructing your layout from scratch. A fully custom design costs considerably more precisely because somebody genuinely is.
* **Who actually writes the words on the finished page.** Genuine copywriting — somebody actually asking what distinguishes your business and then writing that down — consumes genuine time. A considerable proportion of expensive-looking website quotes are really copywriting quotes wearing a web design hat.
* **Whatever the finished site ultimately has to connect to.** A site merely displaying information is substantially simpler than one wired into a booking calendar, a CRM, or online ordering. Every additional integration constitutes further scope, and scope invariably translates into money.

## What you actually get at each price point

**$0–$30 monthly, self-service (Squarespace, Wix, or a Google Business Profile on its own).** Adequate for a business that principally requires an online existence and reasonable findability: hours, location, telephone number, and a few photographs. You are performing the configuration yourself, working within an existing template rather than commissioning anything bespoke.

**$1,500–$6,000 as a one-time build, commissioned from a freelancer or a small shop.** A genuine site constructed for your business specifically: your words, your structure, verified on mobile, and configured so that Google can actually read it. This constitutes the range most small businesses intending to be discovered through search ought to be examining seriously.

**$10,000 and upward, working through an established agency.** You are paying for a team, a process, and account management, rather than necessarily for a better end result than the range above produces. Occasionally worthwhile where you require ongoing strategy alongside the construction; frequently you are simply financing overhead.

## What about AI website builders and "vibe coding"?

This is the portion of the subject that has changed most dramatically across the past year, and equally the portion where any honest advice has to include a frank admission that we do not entirely know yet.

The advantage on offer is entirely genuine: AI tools can generate a working, published website carrying real pages and a functioning contact form within minutes, for close to nothing. For a business that simply needs *something live today*, that constitutes a genuine and defensible option rather than a gimmick worth dismissing out of hand.

The catch appears later, and it appears in a few reasonably specific ways. Security analysis of AI-generated code has found a [2.7 times higher vulnerability density than in human-written code](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.26103), with high-severity vulnerabilities appearing roughly two and a half times as frequently; a scan of approximately 5,600 vibe-coded applications turned up over 2,000 vulnerabilities and more than 400 exposed secrets. Sites built this way additionally tend to accumulate messy, difficult-to-repair structure faster than a site somebody actually planned, which matters on the day you want to add something new and discover the foundation cannot readily support it. And a number of businesses have been caught out by AI-generated legal boilerplate — a cookie notice or privacy page that was invented rather than genuinely accurate to what the site does, which constitutes its own particular variety of legal liability.

None of that establishes that AI-built sites are a bad idea. It establishes that "AI website builder" and "$6,000 custom site" are not genuinely competing for the same customer. One is optimized for getting something live quickly and cheaply today. The other is optimized for holding up as your business grows, as SEO actually has to work, and as somebody needs to repair something six months from now without starting over. Which one suits you depends entirely on which of those problems you actually have.

If your website is a brochure — hours, contact information, a few photographs — then an AI builder or a DIY platform constitutes a completely reasonable place to begin. If your website is intended to be how people *find* you and decide to call, then the SEO groundwork and the writing tend to matter considerably more than they receive credit for, and that is where paying a person still earns its cost.

## A simple way to pick a number

Establish first what the website genuinely needs to accomplish on your behalf. If the honest answer is that it should exist and appear legitimate, budget toward the self-service end of the range. If the honest answer is that it should bring in customers actively searching for what you do, budget for the custom-build range — and ask whoever you hire how they think about search rather than merely about design, before signing anything at all.
