> **TL;DR** — Small Business Saturday, created by American Express in 2010 and held on the Saturday after Thanksgiving, is genuinely useful for a business somebody can walk into and buy something from that day. It does considerably less for a service business whose customers book weeks ahead, and pretending otherwise wastes an afternoon you could spend on something that compounds. If you do participate, the preparation is where the return is: accurate hours, a specific offer, and a listing that works when the extra traffic arrives.

The honest answer to this question depends almost entirely on what kind of business you run, and most advice about it skips that step because the advice is written for retailers.

## What it is, briefly

Small Business Saturday is a shopping day created by American Express in 2010, held on the Saturday following Thanksgiving, positioned between Black Friday and Cyber Monday and intended to direct holiday spending toward independent businesses.

It has become a genuine cultural fixture, which matters because it means some proportion of your neighbors will be looking for somewhere local to spend money that day without needing to be persuaded of the premise.

The scale of that is measurable, and the trend line is worth reading honestly rather than just repeating the round numbers. American Express, which still runs its own Consumer Insights survey on the day every year, reported an estimated $18 billion in consumer spending at small businesses on the 2025 Small Business Saturday — down nearly 20% from an estimated $22 billion in 2024, though still above where the figure stood in 2023. Across the full run of the campaign from 2021 through 2025, American Express reports a cumulative $100 billion in consumer-reported spending, a goal it set for itself and met. Both things are true at once: the day still moves real, substantial money toward small businesses, and the single-year number just went through its sharpest drop in the campaign's recent history. Neither fact cancels the other out, and a business deciding whether to participate should hold both.

## Who it genuinely helps

The businesses that do well are the ones where the mechanism actually works, and the mechanism is walking in and buying.

A shop, a bakery, a bookseller, a restaurant, a gift-adjacent business of almost any kind — for these the day converts existing goodwill into transactions on a specific date, and the marginal effort is small because you are open anyway. If you sell things that people can carry out of the door, this is a reasonable day on which to make an effort.

## Who it mostly does not

For a service business, the day is considerably weaker, and it is worth saying so rather than implying otherwise.

A dentist, a plumber, an accountant, a consultancy — nobody is going to impulse-book a root canal on a Saturday in November because a campaign encouraged them to shop locally. The purchase is considered, scheduled, and frequently insurance-mediated, and none of that responds to a shopping holiday.

That does not mean ignoring the day entirely, but rather that the realistic goal is visibility rather than same-day revenue, and the effort should be proportionate to that.

## If you are participating, this is where the return is

The day rewards preparation and punishes improvisation, and the preparation is unglamorous.

**Confirm your hours are right for that specific Saturday**, using special hours if they differ, and extra foot traffic arriving at a closed door on the one day people made an effort to shop locally is worse than not participating.

**Have one specific thing to offer**, and state it plainly, and "Come support local business" does not constitute an offer in any useful sense. "Every order over $50 on Saturday includes the gift wrapping we normally charge for" is, and the specificity is what makes it repeatable by somebody telling a friend.

**Publish it where people will see it**, which for a local business means the Business Profile as much as social media. A post attached to your listing reaches somebody already looking at you and deciding, which is a better audience than a feed.

**Make sure the listing survives the traffic.** Correct address, tappable phone number, photographs that show what the place actually looks like. The day sends people who have not been before, and those people need directions rather than persuasion.

## What the honest measurement looks like

Count something, whatever it is, and decide in advance what would count as it having been worth it, then check.

That might be transactions on the day against a normal Saturday, or new customers who mentioned it, or simply whether the extra work fitted around actually serving people. A day that produces a queue you could not serve properly is not obviously a success, and a day that produces four new regulars is, even if the takings were unremarkable.

## The alternative use of the same afternoon

If you concluded above that your business is in the second category, here is what to do with the effort instead.

Spend it on the things that work every week rather than one Saturday: ask this month's customers for reviews, correct the three directories with your old phone number, write the page that answers the question people keep ringing to ask, set your special hours for the whole of December.

None of that is festive and all of it compounds, which is the opposite trade from a single day in November. For a service business that is the better deal, and saying so is more useful than encouraging you to decorate a window nobody is going to walk past.
