> **TL;DR** — There is no correction form for an AI assistant's answer, and asking the assistant to remember a correction does not change whatever it tells the next person. The only durable remedy is to correct the sources it draws on: your Business Profile, your own site, and the directories still carrying the obsolete detail. Then wait, because these systems refresh according to a schedule of their own choosing. Note also that the exposure is considerably smaller than it feels, since Pew found that people click a link inside an AI summary on 1% of visits.

This one arrives as a specific and unnerving report: a customer says the assistant told them you close at five, or that you do not accept a particular insurance, or that you are located in a different town. The instinct is to locate the complaints department, and no such department exists anywhere.

## Why you cannot fix the answer directly

Assistants generate a response at the moment of asking, assembled from material gathered beforehand, which means there is no stored sentence about your business available to edit.

Correcting the model inside a conversation does not help either, because telling it that it is wrong may change that particular conversation, but it does not change whatever the next person is told, because the correction was never written anywhere durable.

So the mental model that actually leads somewhere useful points upstream: an assistant is reporting whatever its sources appear to say, and if it is wrong, then some source is wrong, obsolete, or contradicted by another source.

## Find the source before you do anything else

Ask the assistant the same question and, wherever it offers them, examine the citations, because that constitutes the fastest available route to whichever page is carrying the stale detail.

Then search for the incorrect fact directly — the old telephone number, the previous address, the discontinued service — and observe where it continues to appear. The answer is usually a directory nobody has considered in four years, an aggregator that syndicated an obsolete record, or your own site's contact page which was updated everywhere except one location.

Contradiction is the common case rather than pure invention, and when two sources disagree a system has to select one of them, which may well not be the one you would prefer.

## Correct the sources, in order of weight

**The Google Business Profile first**, because it constitutes the structured, verified record and it carries disproportionate weight. Hours, address, telephone, services and attributes all need to be simultaneously accurate and complete.

**Then your own site**, and specifically as text rather than as an image or a PDF. If your hours exist only inside a graphic on the homepage, they are not readable, and a system will instead take them from wherever it can actually read them.

**Then the directories** that still rank when you search your own business name, because those are the ones genuinely being read, and repairing the top handful accomplishes considerably more than attempting an exhaustive sweep.

**Then anywhere the obsolete fact is repeated** — an old press mention, a chamber listing, a supplier's stockist page. Requesting corrections politely is generally sufficient in each case, because the overwhelming majority of them will oblige.

## Then wait, which is the unsatisfying part

These systems refresh according to their own cadence, and there is no button that compels re-crawling of everything that mentions you.

Expect the correction to take effect over weeks rather than hours, and check periodically rather than daily. If a particular assistant offers a feedback mechanism on its answers, use it — it is not a guaranteed route, and it costs nothing whatsoever.

Meanwhile, if the incorrect detail is actively costing you — people arriving after closing, for instance — position the correct information somewhere unavoidable in the interim: a post on your profile, a line at the top of the relevant page.

## Keep it in proportion

Holding the size of this problem accurately is worth the discipline, because the anxiety it produces substantially exceeds its measured impact.

Pew's tracking of 68,879 real searches found that when an AI summary appeared, a click on a source link inside it happened on **1%** of visits. An assistant misstating your hours constitutes a genuine problem, and it is not, on the published evidence, the largest channel through which people currently reach you.

Which points toward the same conclusion as most things on this site: fix the profile, fix the site, fix the three directories carrying the wrong number. That work corrects the assistants as a side effect, because those were the sources they were reading.
