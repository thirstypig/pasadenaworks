> **TL;DR** — There is no correction form for an AI assistant's answer, and asking it to remember a correction does not change what it tells the next person. The only durable fix is to correct the sources it draws on: your Business Profile, your own site, and the directories that still carry the old detail. Then wait, because these systems refresh on their own schedule. Note also that the exposure is smaller than it feels — Pew found people click a link inside an AI summary on 1% of visits.

This one arrives as a specific and unnerving report: a customer says the assistant told them you close at five, or that you do not take a particular insurance, or that you are in a different town. The instinct is to find the complaints department, and there is not one.

## Why you cannot fix the answer directly

Assistants generate a response at the moment of asking, from material gathered beforehand, which means there is no stored sentence about your business to edit.

Correcting the model in a conversation does not help either. Telling it that it is wrong may change that conversation, and it does not change what the next person is told, because the correction was not written anywhere durable.

So the mental model that leads somewhere useful is upstream: an assistant is reporting what its sources appear to say. If it is wrong, some source is wrong, out of date, or contradicted by another.

## Find the source before you do anything else

Ask the assistant the same question and, where it offers them, look at the citations. That is the fastest route to whichever page is carrying the stale detail.

Then search for the incorrect fact directly — the old telephone number, the previous address, the discontinued service — and see where it still appears. The answer is usually a directory nobody has thought about in four years, an aggregator that syndicated an old record, or your own site's contact page which was updated everywhere except one place.

Contradiction is the common case rather than pure invention. When two sources disagree, a system has to pick, and it may not pick the one you would prefer.

## Correct the sources, in order of weight

**The Google Business Profile first**, because it is the structured, verified record and it carries disproportionate weight. Hours, address, phone, services, attributes — all of it accurate and complete.

**Then your own site**, and specifically as text rather than as an image or a PDF. If your hours live only in a graphic on the homepage, they are not readable, and a system will take them from wherever it can read them instead.

**Then the directories** that still rank when you search your own business name. Those are the ones actually being read. Fix the top handful rather than attempting an exhaustive sweep.

**Then anywhere the old fact is repeated** — an old press mention, a chamber listing, a supplier's stockist page. Ask for corrections politely; most will oblige.

## Then wait, which is the unsatisfying part

These systems refresh on their own cadence, and there is no button that forces re-crawling of everything that mentions you.

Expect the correction to take effect over weeks rather than hours, and check periodically rather than daily. If a particular assistant offers a feedback mechanism on its answers, use it — it is not a guaranteed route, and it costs nothing.

Meanwhile, if the wrong detail is actively costing you — people arriving after closing, say — put the correct information somewhere unavoidable in the interim: a post on your profile, a line at the top of the relevant page.

## Keep it in proportion

It is worth holding the size of this problem accurately, because the anxiety it produces exceeds its measured impact.

Pew's tracking of 68,879 real searches found that when an AI summary appeared, a click on a source link inside it happened on **1%** of visits. An assistant misstating your hours is a genuine problem and it is not, on the published evidence, the largest channel through which people currently reach you.

Which points at the same conclusion as most things on this site: fix the profile, fix the site, fix the three directories with the wrong number. That work corrects the assistants as a side effect, because those are the sources they were reading.
