> **TL;DR** — The Rose Parade and Rose Bowl Game close streets, flood the area around the route with visitors, and spike local search volume every late December and on January 1st. If you are situated near Colorado Boulevard, or simply in Pasadena generally, update your Google Business Profile hours, publish genuine access and parking information before the final week of December, and decide now whether you are staffing up or closing down for parade morning.

If your business sits anywhere near Pasadena, the final week of December and January 1st do not constitute a normal week. Colorado Boulevard closes for route setup and cleanup, hotels within a few miles book out months ahead, and searches for "open near Rose Parade," "parking Rose Parade," and "restaurants near Colorado Blvd" climb sharply across the fortnight preceding New Year's Day. If you have not touched your Google Business Profile since October, you are surrendering easy visibility during the single week of the year when the greatest number of out-of-town people are actively searching for somewhere near you.

## What actually changes in December

The [Tournament of Roses parade](https://www.visitpasadena.com/events/annual-events/rose-parade-guide/) covers five and a half miles, and the direction matters if you are working out whether it reaches you. It begins at Green Street and Orange Grove Boulevard, proceeds north along Orange Grove at roughly two and a half miles per hour, turns **east** onto Colorado Boulevard, where the overwhelming majority of viewing occurs, and finally turns north onto Sierra Madre Boulevard before concluding at Villa Street.

It runs on January 1st unless that date falls on a Sunday, in which case it moves to January 2nd — a "[Never on Sunday](https://ksltv.com/national-news/rose-bowl-parade-continues-its-never-on-sunday-tradition-established-in-1893/516105/)" rule established in 1893, when organizers worried the commotion would frighten horses tied outside churches and disrupt services. It has applied roughly twenty times since, and the next occurrence is not until 2034, so for planning purposes you can assume January 1st for the foreseeable future.

The city closes sections of the route the night before for float staging, and grandstand teardown commences the same day. If your business sits on or near the route — Old Pasadena, the stretch of Colorado through the Playhouse District, anywhere within a few blocks of Orange Grove — expect restricted vehicle access for at least twenty-four to forty-eight hours around New Year's.

The Rose Bowl Game contributes a second surge, because game-day traffic backs up the 210 and the surface streets near the stadium for hours before kickoff, and in years when the Rose Bowl hosts a College Football Playoff semifinal, both hotel occupancy and search interest rise earlier and persist longer.

Search behavior follows the identical curve. From mid-December onward, "near Rose Parade," "hotels near Rose Parade route," and "restaurants open New Year's Day Pasadena" all climb, and Google leans heavily on Business Profile data — hours, popular times, photographs — when deciding who appears for those searches. A profile carrying stale holiday hours or no recent photographs loses to a competitor who updated theirs.

## Your December checklist

**Update Google Business Profile hours by December 15.** Do not wait until Christmas week. Set your genuine hours for December 24th, 25th, 31st, and January 1st, including whether you are closing early or remaining open for the crowds. An out-of-date "open" status while you are actually closed damages your rating considerably more than being accurately closed does.

**Publish an update about parade-week access.** If customers need to know that a street will be closed, or that parking near you disappears for two days, say so in a Business Profile post or a banner on your site. People searching "near Rose Parade" during parade week are frequently visiting the area for the first time, and they do not know your neighborhood's ordinary traffic pattern, let alone its parade-week one.

**Decide on staffing before December 20.** If you are on or near the route, parade week can represent either your strongest sales day of the year or a day when you cannot get staff or product into the building at all. Establish which applies to you and staff accordingly, rather than discovering at six in the morning on January 1st that half your crew cannot get through the street closures.

**Confirm your parking and access information is current.** If you have a lot, say so. If street parking near you gets suspended, indicate where the nearest alternative sits. This is boring, unglamorous copy — a paragraph rather than a campaign — but it is precisely what somebody standing on a closed street at seven in the morning is attempting to find on their phone.

## What's not worth doing

Do not build a special "Rose Parade" landing page consisting of generic parade history and no genuine information about your business. Google can distinguish between a page answering whether this business is open and how to reach it, and a page padded with facts about the Tournament of Roses copied from somewhere else. If you have nothing specific and true to say about parade week — your actual hours, your actual access situation — then a Business Profile post does the job considerably better than an entire page.

Do not assume the surge automatically locates you. Sitting three blocks from the route places you in front of nobody unless your listing is accurate and current at the moment they are searching. The businesses that benefit from parade week are those that spent twenty minutes in December ensuring Google held the right information, rather than those with the most elaborate float-themed decor.
