> **TL;DR** — The Rose Parade and Rose Bowl Game close streets, flood the area around the route with people, and spike local search volume every late December and January 1st. If you're near Colorado Boulevard or just in Pasadena generally, update your Google Business Profile hours, post real access/parking info before the last week of December, and decide now whether you're staffing up or closing down for parade morning.

If your business is anywhere near Pasadena, the last week of December and January 1st are not a normal week. Colorado Boulevard closes for the parade route setup and cleanup, hotels within a few miles book out months in advance, and searches for "open near Rose Parade," "parking Rose Parade," and "restaurants near Colorado Blvd" spike hard in the two weeks before New Year's Day. If you haven't touched your Google Business Profile since October, you're leaving easy visibility on the table during the one week of the year when the most out-of-town people are actively searching for a place near you.

## What actually changes in December

The Tournament of Roses parade runs down Colorado Boulevard from Sierra Madre Villa Avenue to Orange Grove Boulevard on January 1st (or January 2nd if the 1st falls on a Sunday). The city closes sections of the route the night before for float staging, and grandstand teardown starts the same day. If your business sits on or near the route — Old Pasadena, the stretch of Colorado through the Playhouse District, anywhere within a few blocks of Orange Grove — expect restricted vehicle access for at least 24 to 48 hours around New Year's.

The Rose Bowl Game adds a second surge. Game day traffic backs up the 210 and surface streets near the stadium for hours before kickoff, and in years the Rose Bowl hosts a College Football Playoff semifinal, hotel occupancy and search interest both jump earlier and last longer.

Search behavior follows the same curve. Starting in mid-December, "near Rose Parade," "hotels near Rose Parade route," and "restaurants open New Year's Day Pasadena" all climb — and Google leans hard on Business Profile data (hours, popular times, photos) to decide who shows up for those searches. A profile with stale holiday hours or no recent photos loses to a competitor who updated theirs.

## Your December checklist

**Update Google Business Profile hours by December 15.** Don't wait until Christmas week. Set your actual hours for December 24, 25, 31, and January 1 — including if you're closing early or staying open for the crowds. An out-of-date "open" status when you're actually closed is worse for your rating than being accurately closed.

**Post an update about parade-week access.** If customers need to know a street will be closed, or that parking near you disappears for two days, say so in a Google Business Profile post or a banner on your site. People searching "near Rose Parade" during parade week are often visiting the area for the first time — they don't know your neighborhood's normal traffic pattern, let alone its parade-week one.

**Decide on staffing before December 20.** If you're in or near the route, parade week can mean your best sales day of the year or a day you can't get staff or product to the building at all. Figure out which one applies to you and staff accordingly — don't find out at 6 a.m. on January 1st that half your crew can't get through the street closures.

**Check your parking and access info is current.** If you have a lot, tell people. If street parking near you gets suspended, say where the nearest alternative is. This is boring, unglamorous copy — a paragraph, not a campaign — but it's exactly what someone standing on a closed street at 7 a.m. is trying to find on their phone.

## What's not worth doing

Don't build a special "Rose Parade" landing page with generic parade history and no real information about your business. Google can tell the difference between a page that answers "is this business open and how do I get there" and a page padded with facts about the Tournament of Roses copied from somewhere else. If you don't have something specific and true to say about parade week — your actual hours, your actual access situation — a Business Profile post does the job better than a whole page.

Don't assume the surge automatically finds you. Being three blocks from the route doesn't put you in front of anyone unless your listing is accurate and current when they're searching. The businesses that benefit from parade week are the ones that spent twenty minutes in December making sure Google had the right information, not the ones with the fanciest float-themed decor.
