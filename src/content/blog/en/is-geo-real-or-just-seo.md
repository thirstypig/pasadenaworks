> **TL;DR** — The underlying shift is real, in that assistants now answer questions people used to answer by clicking a result. The discipline being sold on the back of it is mostly not, because Google's own documentation states that appearing in its AI features requires no additional work beyond ordinary search fundamentals, and specifically no new files or markup. Where the label describes measuring differently, it is useful. Where it describes a separate technical service with its own invoice line, ask to see the requirement written down.

Every few years the search industry produces an acronym, and the acronym arrives attached to a proposal. The useful question is never whether the acronym is fashionable; it is whether the work it names is different from work you were already paying for.

## The part that is genuinely new

It would be wrong to dismiss the whole subject, because something did change and it changed recently.

People increasingly ask a question and receive an answer rather than a list, which means a proportion of the traffic that used to arrive on your site now resolves before anybody clicks anything. That has consequences for how you measure success, for what a "good" ranking is worth, and for how much of your value has to be visible in a short extracted passage rather than three paragraphs down a page.

Those are real changes and they deserve genuine attention, although it is worth noticing that all three are changes in *measurement and emphasis* rather than in the technical work.

## The part that is being oversold

Here the vendor's claim and the platform's documentation are in direct conflict, which is a rare and useful position for a buyer.

Google states plainly that there are "no additional requirements to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode, nor other special optimizations necessary," and goes further: "you don't need to create new machine readable files, AI text files, or markup to appear in these features," adding that there is no special schema.org structured data to add either.

That is about as direct a contradiction of the "GEO requires a new technical layer" pitch as a platform is ever likely to publish. It does not cover every assistant on the market, for the straightforward reason that Google documents Google. It nonetheless means that a proposal built around special files for AI visibility is, at minimum, undocumented, and the person proposing it should be able to say where the requirement comes from.

## What the honest version of the work looks like

Strip out the acronym and a defensible engagement in this area contains a handful of items, most of which predate the acronym by fifteen years.

Content that answers a specific question directly, near the top of the page, in language somebody would actually use — this helps an extraction system and a hurried human identically. Facts about the business should be stated as text rather than merely implied by the design. Accurate, consistent details wherever the business appears, since contradictory information gives no system a basis for confidence. Crawlability, including a deliberate decision about which assistant crawlers you permit rather than an accidental one inherited from an old `robots.txt`.

And then measurement that acknowledges the shift: fewer clicks for the same visibility is now an expected outcome rather than a failure, and reporting that treats it as failure will push you toward the wrong decisions.

## How to evaluate a proposal

Ask the following three questions, and the answers will sort a serious proposal from a repackaged one considerably faster than any amount of comparison shopping.

First, which specific tasks in this proposal would not have appeared in an SEO proposal in 2019? If the honest answer is none, the pricing should reflect that rather than the novelty. Second, where exactly is each requirement documented by the platform it purports to apply to, bearing in mind that the acceptable answer is a documentation page rather than a confident tone. Third, what does success look like, and does the measure account for answers that resolve without a click?

A vendor who answers all three well is worth listening to, and quite possibly worth hiring. The answers themselves will prove considerably more informative to you than the acronym ever was.

## The summary worth carrying

Something did change, the change is mostly about measurement and emphasis, and the technical fundamentals are the ones you already knew about.

Anybody selling a separate discipline should be asked to point at the documentation, and the most likely outcome of that request is a considerably more modest and more accurate description of what the work actually involves. That is a thoroughly good outcome for a buyer, and obtaining it costs nothing beyond a single question asked at the right moment.
