> **TL;DR** — Set your special hours for every closure between now and January, in advance, rather than the morning somebody arrives at a locked door. Send yourself a test enquiry through your own contact form, check what your site does on a phone over a slow connection, and decide now what you will do when you are too busy to answer the telephone. None of these takes an hour, and each of them is considerably more expensive to discover in December.

The fourth quarter punishes the things you have been meaning to get to, because volume discovers every weakness simultaneously and there is no opportunity to repair anything while it is happening. September and early October constitute the period when this work remains inexpensive.

## 1. Set special hours now, for every closure

Google supports special hours for dates on which you are open at different times or closed entirely, and establishing them is a five-minute operation you can complete for the entire quarter in a single sitting.

Do Thanksgiving, the days around Christmas, New Year's Eve and New Year's Day, plus any closure specific to your business — the week you always take, the day of the staff party, the afternoon you close for stocktaking. A profile displaying normal hours on a day you are closed produces an irritated customer and, quite frequently, a review documenting the experience.

This is comfortably the highest-return item on the entire list, and it is simultaneously the one most often left undone until somebody eventually complains about it.

## 2. Test your own contact form

Send yourself an enquiry through your own website, from your phone, in the way a customer would, and then confirm that it arrives somewhere a person will actually read during a busy week.

Contact forms characteristically fail in silence, continuing to display a cheerful confirmation while delivering nothing whatsoever, and the failure remains invisible until you notice enquiries have stopped — which in a quiet month can take weeks. In December that same delay costs you the entire season.

While you are there, establish where the messages actually go, because a form delivering to an address nobody opens is functionally identical to a broken one.

## 3. Look at your site on a phone, on a bad connection

Your customers in December are looking at your site in a car park, on a phone, with one bar, while deciding whether to drive to you.

Load it under those conditions rather than on the office wifi, time how long it takes before anything useful appears, and confirm that the telephone number is tappable, that the hours are visible without extensive scrolling, and that the address appears as text a map application can actually act upon.

Anything that has accumulated on the site since last year — a chat widget, a booking embed, a tracking script, a pop-up — is costing you load time on exactly the device that matters.

## 4. Decide what happens when you cannot answer

The busiest days are precisely the ones on which the phone rings while you are already with a customer, and the default outcome in that situation is that the caller goes to a competitor instead.

Decide the matter in advance rather than in the moment, whether that means a voicemail message that actually says when you will call back, a note on the profile explaining that email gets a faster answer this month, or a person whose job on Saturdays is the phone. Any of those is better than the version where nobody decides and the calls simply go unanswered.

## 5. Check what your listing says you sell

Seasonal businesses change what they offer as the year turns, and they frequently forget to announce it anywhere a customer would actually see it.

If you take orders with a deadline, publish the deadline, and if something is unavailable until January, say that rather than letting somebody arrive for it, and if you add something for the season, add it to the services on your profile, since a service you have not listed is one you cannot be found for.

## 6. Write down this year's numbers while they are in front of you

At the end of the quarter, before the details evaporate, note what actually happened: which weeks were busiest, what people asked for, what you ran out of, what you turned away.

Use Search Console's performance report to see which queries brought people in during the season, since that record is genuinely useful and nobody remembers it in June. A page of notes written in early January is what makes next year's version of this checklist specific to your business rather than generic.

## The one that is not on the list

Do not begin a new marketing initiative of any kind during the fourth quarter.

A new website, a new booking system, a rebrand or a first advertising campaign are all reasonable projects and all wrong for the season, because the cost of something going wrong reaches its annual maximum precisely when your available attention reaches its minimum. Do the six items above, get through the quarter, and start the interesting work in January when a mistake is survivable.
