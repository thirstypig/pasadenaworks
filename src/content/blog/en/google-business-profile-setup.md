> **TL;DR** — Search "your business name" on Google, click "Own this business?" or "Claim this business," and verify with whatever method Google offers you — usually video now, sometimes an instant Search Console check. Postcard verification is mostly gone as of July 2026. Once you're in, the real work starts: most owners claim their profile and then leave it half-filled, which is worse for ranking than not having one polished.

Maybe your business doesn't show up on Google Maps when someone searches for it. Maybe it shows up, but someone else's placeholder listing is sitting there instead. Either way, you claim it the same way: search your business name on Google or in Google Maps, and look for "Own this business?" or "Claim this business." Click it. That starts the process. The part people get stuck on is verification, and the part they skip entirely is fixing what's actually wrong with the listing once they're in.

## Claiming and verifying, step by step

Once you click to claim, Google walks you through confirming your business details — name, address, phone, category — and then makes you prove you're really the owner. That's verification, and it's not optional.

**Video verification is the default now for most categories.** Google added this specifically to cut down on fake listings, and by mid-2026 it's what most small businesses get offered first. You walk around your location on camera showing the signage, the interior, proof you're standing where you say your business is. Takes about five minutes to record if you're prepared; Google usually reviews it within two to five business days.

**Postcard verification is mostly retired.** As of July 2026, Google stopped mailing verification postcards to most businesses. If you've read an older guide — including some published earlier this year — that describes waiting two weeks for a postcard, that's out of date for most categories now. A few edge cases still use it, but don't plan around it.

**Phone, text, or email verification** still shows up for some businesses, and it's the easiest version: a code arrives in minutes, you type it in, done.

**Instant verification through Search Console** is the fastest path if you qualify — it happens if you've already verified your business's website domain in Google Search Console under the same account. Worth checking before you do anything else, since it skips the wait entirely.

Whichever method Google offers you, it's offered based on your category and location, not something you get to pick. Use what's in front of you.

## What people get wrong after claiming it

Claiming the profile is the easy part. Here's what actually determines whether it helps you or just sits there.

**The primary category is doing more work than owners think.** It's one of the strongest ranking signals Google Business Profile has, and a lot of businesses pick something too broad — "Marketing Agency" instead of "SEO Agency," "Contractor" instead of "Kitchen Remodeler." A vague category means Google shows you for vague searches, which is exactly the searches you don't win. Pick the most specific category that's still true, not the one that sounds most impressive.

**Hours that don't match your website confuse Google, not just customers.** If your profile says you close at 5 and your site says 6, or your holiday hours never get updated, Google's confidence in the listing's accuracy drops — and so does how often it gets shown. Update both places at the same time, every time, or don't bother updating either.

**Service areas set by hope instead of reality.** If you serve San Gabriel Valley cities but don't have a public storefront, Google lets you list a service area instead of a street address. The mistake is loading it up with every city you'd like to work in someday instead of the ones you actually serve now. A service area padded with wishful cities reads as padded to Google too, and dilutes the ranking signal for the places you really cover. List where you do the work, not where you hope to.

**A thin photo set reads as an inactive business.** A profile with three photos from the day it was created looks abandoned, even if the business behind it is thriving. Interior shots, exterior shots with your signage visible, photos of actual work — not stock images — tell Google and searchers that someone's still paying attention here.

None of this is a one-time fix. A profile that's accurate today and never touched again slowly drifts out of date the same way any unmaintained listing does. The claiming and verifying part takes an afternoon. Keeping it honest is the part that actually pays off.
