> **TL;DR** — Search your business name on Google, select "Own this business?" or "Claim this business," and complete whichever verification method Google presents to you. Recorded video has become the default route for most categories, though Google continues to document mail, phone, text, email, and live video call as alternatives. The harder problem arrives afterward: most owners claim a profile and then abandon it half-completed, which serves them considerably worse than a modest profile maintained honestly.

Perhaps your business fails to appear on Google Maps when a customer searches for it by name. Perhaps it appears, but an unclaimed placeholder listing occupies the position that should be yours. The remedy is identical in either case: search your business name on Google or within Google Maps, and look for "Own this business?" or "Claim this business." Selecting that link begins the process. Where owners typically stall is verification, and what they omit almost universally is correcting whatever is actually wrong with the listing once access has been granted.

## Claiming and verifying, step by step

Having initiated the claim, Google walks you through confirming your business details — name, address, telephone, category — and then requires you to demonstrate that you genuinely own the business. That demonstration is verification, and there is no route around it.

**Recorded video verification has become the default for most categories.** Google introduced it specifically to suppress fraudulent listings, and by 2026 it is what most small businesses are offered first. You record an unedited walkthrough of your location showing signage, interior, and whatever else establishes that you are standing where you claim to operate. Recording it takes roughly five minutes if you have prepared; Google [documents a standard five-business-day review period](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7107242) once a method has been completed.

**Mail verification still exists, contrary to a good deal of published advice.** Video became the recommended and most frequently offered route during 2026, and postcards now surface far less often than they once did — particularly for service-area businesses without a storefront. But Google's own documentation continues to list mail among the available methods, so the widely repeated claim that postcards have been discontinued should be treated as an overstatement rather than as fact, which in practice means planning around video without assuming that mail has vanished entirely.

**Phone, text, and email verification** remain available to some businesses, and they are by far the least demanding of the routes on offer: a code arrives within minutes, you enter it, and the matter is settled.

**A live video call** is a distinct option from the recorded walkthrough, one in which a support representative joins you in real time rather than reviewing a file afterward. Owners frequently conflate the two and then find themselves unprepared when an actual person appears on the call.

**Instant verification through Search Console** is the quickest path available to those who qualify, and eligibility depends on having already verified your website's domain in Google Search Console under the same account. It is worth checking before attempting anything else, because it bypasses the waiting period altogether.

Whichever method Google presents, that presentation is determined by your category and location rather than by your preference, so the practical instruction is simply to use whatever you are given.

## What people get wrong after claiming it

Claiming a profile is the straightforward part, and what follows it is what determines whether the listing works on your behalf or merely occupies space.

**The primary category carries more weight than most owners assume.** Google [names category among the inputs to relevance](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091) — one of the three factors, alongside distance and prominence, that determine local ranking. Many businesses nonetheless select something too broad: "Marketing Agency" rather than "SEO Agency," "Contractor" rather than "Kitchen Remodeler." A vague category invites Google to surface you for vague searches, which are precisely the searches you lose. Select the most specific category that remains truthful, rather than the one that sounds most impressive.

**Hours that contradict your website undermine Google's confidence, not merely your customers'.** Where your profile closes at five and your site says six, or where holiday hours are never revised, the inconsistency degrades how reliable the listing appears — and Google lists accurate hours among the same relevance inputs. Update both locations simultaneously, every time, or decline to update either.

**Service areas defined by aspiration rather than reality.** If you serve San Gabriel Valley cities without operating a public storefront, Google permits a service area in place of a street address. The error lies in populating it with every city you might eventually wish to work in, instead of those you genuinely serve now. A service area padded with aspirational cities reads as padded to Google as well, diluting the signal for the places you actually cover. List where the work happens, not where you hope it will.

**A thin photo set reads as an inactive business.** A profile carrying three images from the day it was created appears abandoned, however thriving the business behind it may be. Interior shots, exterior shots with visible signage, and photographs of genuine work — never stock imagery — demonstrate to Google and to searchers alike that somebody is still paying attention.

None of this constitutes a one-time repair, because a profile that is accurate today and never revisited afterward drifts out of date exactly as any other unmaintained listing does. Claiming and verifying occupies a single afternoon. Keeping the thing honest is the part that actually pays.
