> **TL;DR** — Google provides special hours for dates when you open at different times or close entirely, and setting them for the whole season takes about ten minutes. Doing it prevents the specific, entirely avoidable failure where somebody reads "Open" on their phone, drives across town on a public holiday, and finds a locked door — which reliably produces a one-star review that outlives the holiday by years. Set them in advance, set them for every date, and check them afterwards on a phone rather than in the dashboard.

This is the least interesting article on this site and possibly the most useful one, because the work takes minutes, the failure it prevents is severe, and an enormous number of businesses do not do it.

## What special hours actually are

Your profile carries regular weekly hours, and separately it supports special hours for specific dates when those regular hours do not apply.

That covers both closures and changes: shut entirely on Christmas Day, opening late on New Year's Day, closing early on Christmas Eve, or the odd Tuesday in January when you take stock. Google documents how to set these, and the mechanism exists precisely because regular weekly hours cannot express a holiday.

The important property is that special hours override the weekly ones for the dates you set, and Google shows them to somebody looking at your listing on that day. That is what stops the profile confidently telling a customer you are open when you are not.

## Why this specific failure is so expensive

The cost is out of proportion to the effort, and understanding why makes it easier to actually do.

Somebody checking your hours on a public holiday is, by definition, making a special trip, and they have chosen you over an alternative, driven somewhere, quite possibly with a family in the car, on a day when their time is scarce. Finding a dark shop is not a minor inconvenience to that person; it is a wasted part of a holiday.

That is the emotional state in which people write reviews, and a one-star review saying "closed despite Google saying open" is unusually damaging because it is unanswerable. You cannot claim it did not happen, the reader recognizes the experience immediately, and it sits on the listing long after the holiday.

## Do the whole season in one sitting

The reason this gets missed is that it is done one date at a time, when somebody remembers, which means the last few never happen.

Sit down once, in November, with a calendar in front of you, and enter every date between now and mid-January where you are closed or on different hours: Thanksgiving and the day after, Christmas Eve, Christmas Day, Boxing Day if relevant, New Year's Eve, New Year's Day, and any closure specific to you — the week you always take, the day of the staff lunch, the afternoon you shut for inventory.

Ten minutes of work, done once, covers the entire period, and setting a recurring reminder for early November each year and the problem is permanently solved.

## Check it the way a customer would

Setting the hours and confirming they display are two different things, and the second takes thirty seconds.

Search your own business on your phone, signed out, and look at what the listing says, and dashboards show you what you entered; a phone shows you what a customer sees, and those occasionally differ — particularly if you have more than one listing, which is more common than people expect and worth discovering now rather than on 24 December.

If you discover a duplicate listing carrying the wrong hours, deal with it before the season begins rather than in the middle of it.

## While you are in there

Two adjacent things are worth the extra five minutes, since you are already logged in.

**Check the phone number and address**, because a holiday is when people ring rather than email, and an old number on a listing is the same as being closed.

**Post the closure as well as setting the hours.** A short post saying "closed 25–26 December, back on the 27th" reaches somebody reading your listing in a way a grayed-out hours row does not, and it is the same information stated twice, which is appropriate for information this consequential.

## The version of this that applies all year

The habit worth building is smaller than a holiday routine: whenever your hours change for any reason, change them in the profile that day rather than intending to.

Businesses reliably update the sign on the door and forget the listing, which means the listing — the version most customers actually consult — is the one that is wrong. Holidays are simply the occasion on which that particular gap costs you the most.
