> **TL;DR** — Sometimes, no. If you're a very visual, referral-driven business and Instagram is already bringing you customers, it can carry you further than you'd think. But you're renting that audience, not owning it, and Google still treats a real website as a stronger trust signal than a profile page. Most businesses need both, not one or the other.

Sometimes, honestly, no. If you make custom cakes, tattoo, or do floral design, and people already find you through Instagram and DM you to book, adding a website might not move the needle much right now. But for most small businesses in Pasadena and the San Gabriel Valley — the plumber, the tax preparer, the physical therapist, the taco spot people search for by name — Instagram alone leaves real money and real customers on the table. Here's the honest version of why, not the scary-story version.

## What changed: Google now indexes some Instagram posts

Since mid-2025, Google started indexing public posts from Instagram Business and Creator accounts — Reels, carousels, photos, and captions can show up in regular search results, not just inside Instagram's own app. That's a real shift, and it's the strongest argument for "Instagram might be enough" that exists right now.

But read the fine print. Only public posts from Business or Creator accounts qualify. Stories never show up. Posts from before January 2020 don't count. And when a post does surface in Google, it's competing on Instagram's domain authority, formatted the way Instagram wants it formatted, with an "Add on Instagram" wrapper around it. It's not a page you control — you can't structure it around the exact question a customer is asking, and your phone number and hours aren't right there. Getting indexed occasionally is not the same as being findable reliably. A website page targeting "emergency plumber Pasadena" and built to answer that question will out-rank a scrolling caption almost every time.

## The part nobody puts in the pitch deck: you don't own the account

Meta bans and locks accounts constantly, and mostly for reasons that have nothing to do with you doing anything wrong — a shared IP address with a flagged account, a connected third-party app, a burst of spam reports from a competitor. Some accounts come back within 48 hours through an appeal. Some never come back, no matter how many appeals go in. If that account is also your only web presence — your only phone number, your only hours, your only proof you exist — a ban isn't an inconvenience. It's your business going dark until Meta feels like fixing it, on Meta's timeline, not yours.

A website doesn't have that failure mode. Nobody can ban your domain because an algorithm got twitchy. That alone is worth the modest cost of hosting for most businesses that depend on being found.

## Credibility is measurable, and it's not close

This isn't a vibe. Recent consumer research (DreamHost's 2026 Local Business Trust Index) found people rate businesses with a website as 41% more trustworthy than those without one. An older but consistent Verisign survey put it at 84% of U.S. consumers viewing a business with a website as more credible than one running on social media alone. People trust content on a company's own site more than they trust the same content on a social platform — even when it's the exact same claim.

That gap matters most at the moment someone is deciding whether to call you back. An Instagram profile can look great and still leave a first-time customer wondering if you're actually a real, established operation, or a side project. A website with your address, your services, and reviews on it answers that question before they finish reading it.

## When Instagram-only genuinely is enough

To be fair to the other side: say your business is intensely visual, sells almost entirely through referral and word of mouth, and your customers are already comfortable booking through a DM or a link in your bio. A full website can be a lower priority than other things you could spend that money on. A one-person custom framer with a two-month waitlist doesn't need to outrank anyone on Google. They need Instagram to keep doing what it's already doing.

The test isn't "do you have Instagram." It's "if Instagram disappeared tomorrow, would anyone still be able to find you, call you, or book you." If the answer is no, that's not an Instagram problem. That's a website problem wearing an Instagram costume.
