> **TL;DR** — Sometimes, no. If yours is an intensely visual, referral-driven business and Instagram is already delivering customers, it can carry you considerably further than you would expect. You are nonetheless renting that audience rather than owning it, and Google continues to treat an independent website as a stronger trust signal than a profile page. Most businesses require both, rather than one in place of the other.

Sometimes, honestly, no: if you produce custom cakes, tattoo, or arrange flowers, and customers already discover you through Instagram and message you to book, then adding a website may not move very much in the immediate term. For most small businesses in Pasadena and the San Gabriel Valley, however — the plumber, the tax preparer, the physical therapist, the taco counter that people search for by name — relying on Instagram alone leaves genuine money and genuine customers unclaimed. What follows is the honest account of why, rather than the frightening one.

## What changed: Google now indexes some Instagram posts

Beginning [10 July 2025](https://ppc.land/instagram-content-becomes-searchable-on-google-starting-july-10/), Google began indexing public posts from Instagram Business and Creator accounts, which means that Reels, carousels, photographs and captions can surface in ordinary search results rather than exclusively within Instagram's own application. That constitutes a genuine shift, and it represents the strongest argument currently available for the proposition that Instagram alone might be sufficient for a small business that photographs well.

The qualifications deserve attention, however. Only public posts from Business or Creator accounts are eligible, Stories never appear at all, and posts predating January 2020 are excluded. Furthermore, when a post does surface in Google, it competes from Instagram's domain, formatted according to Instagram's preferences, wrapped in an "Add on Instagram" frame. It is not a page you control: you cannot organize it around the precise question a customer is asking, and neither your telephone number nor your opening hours accompany it. Being indexed occasionally is not equivalent to being reliably discoverable. A website page constructed to answer "emergency plumber Pasadena" will outrank a scrolling caption on almost every occasion.

## The part nobody puts in the pitch deck: you don't own the account

Meta suspends and locks accounts continually, and predominantly for reasons entirely unconnected to any wrongdoing on your part — an IP address shared with a flagged account, a connected third-party application, or a burst of spam reports originating with a competitor. Some accounts are restored within forty-eight hours through an appeal. Others never return, irrespective of how many appeals are submitted. Where that account also constitutes your only web presence — your only telephone number, your only opening hours, your only evidence that you exist — a suspension is not an inconvenience. It is your business going dark until Meta elects to address it, on Meta's schedule rather than on yours.

A website carries no equivalent failure mode, because nobody is in a position to suspend your domain simply because an algorithm turned twitchy. That consideration alone justifies the modest cost of hosting for any business that depends upon being found.

## Credibility is measurable, and it's not close

This is not merely an impression, and the figures are unusually consistent across a decade of surveying. [DreamHost's 2026 Local Business Trust Index](https://www.dreamhost.com/blog/dreamhost-2026-local-business-trust-index/), which surveyed more than 1,200 U.S. consumers, found that people rate businesses possessing a website as 41% more trustworthy than those without one — the strongest credibility signal the study identified apart from online reviews. An older but entirely consistent [Verisign survey from 2015](https://www.verisign.com/assets/ebook-5ReasonsSMBWebsite-Jan2016.pdf) placed the figure at 84% of U.S. consumers regarding a business with a website as more credible than one operating exclusively through social media. People demonstrably trust material on a company's own site more than they trust identical material on a social platform, even where the claim itself is word for word the same.

That disparity matters most at precisely the moment somebody is deciding whether to return your call. An Instagram profile can look accomplished and nonetheless leave a first-time customer uncertain whether you constitute a genuinely established operation or somebody's weekend project. A website carrying your address, your services and your reviews resolves that question before the reader has finished with it.

## When Instagram-only genuinely is enough

In fairness to the opposing case: suppose your business is intensely visual, sells almost entirely through referral and word of mouth, and your customers are already comfortable booking through a direct message or a link in your bio. A complete website may then rank below other things that money could reasonably be spent on. A sole custom framer carrying a two-month waiting list has no need to outrank anybody on Google, and requires only that Instagram continue doing what it already does.

The test is not whether you have Instagram, but whether, supposing Instagram vanished tomorrow, anybody could still find you, telephone you, or book you. Where the answer is no, that is not an Instagram problem. That is a website problem wearing an Instagram costume.
